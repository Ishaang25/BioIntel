## Your task

The numbers are already set. Your job is to make them mean something.

**`headline`** — one sentence an IC chair could read aloud to open the discussion. It must
carry the overall score as given and characterise the *shape* of the risk, not just its
size. "The science is credible but almost none of the regulatory narrative can be verified
from public sources" is a headline; "the score is 72/100" is not.

**`commentary`** — one entry per dimension, in the order supplied, echoing the dimension
key exactly.

- `so_what` — the consequence of this score for the decision. Not a restatement. If
  clinical maturity is 74 and translational readiness is 50, the so-what is that the
  programmes are further along than their supporting evidence justifies, and the risk sits
  in the gap.
- `what_would_change_it` — the specific evidence that would move this score materially. Be
  concrete enough to put in a data-room request.

For a dimension marked not assessed, say what was missing from the deck and whether that
absence is itself informative. A company that says nothing about manufacturing at Phase 3
is telling you something; one that says nothing at discovery stage is not.

**`decisive_factors`** — the 2-4 findings that most determine the recommendation, each
phrased as a consequence. "Three thesis-critical regulatory claims rest on company
assertion alone, so the diligence path runs through agency correspondence rather than
literature" is decisive; "several claims were unverified" is not.

## Discipline

- Never contradict a supplied number, and never compute a new one.
- Distinguish "scored low because evidence disagrees" from "scored low because the claim
  type carries a low prior" from "not assessed because nothing informed it". These have
  completely different implications and the memo must not blur them.
- Where a dimension scores low because claims could not be *checked* rather than because
  they were *refuted*, say so explicitly. That is an information problem with a clear
  remedy, and it should read differently from an evidence problem.
