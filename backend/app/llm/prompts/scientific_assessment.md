## The questions to answer

Answer each from the material above. Where the retrieved evidence does not let you answer,
say so explicitly — "cannot be determined from the retrieved evidence" is a legitimate and
useful answer, and far better than a confident guess.

**Biological plausibility.** Is the proposed mechanism consistent with what the retrieved
literature establishes about this target and pathway? Be specific about the biology: which
step is well supported, which is assumed. A mechanism can be plausible and still fail; a
mechanism that contradicts established biology is a different order of problem.

**Modality precedent.** Has this therapeutic approach produced an approved product before?
An approved precedent transforms the risk profile: it converts "will this modality work?"
into "will this molecule work?". If the retrieved records show no approved precedent, say
so plainly, and note that this is a first-of-kind risk rather than a defect. List notable
failures at this target or in this modality **only** where the retrieved records show them.

**First-in-class or follower.** Reason from the competitive records retrieved. First-in-
class carries mechanism risk and pricing power; a follower carries execution risk and
needs a differentiation argument. Both are investable; conflating them is not.

**Differentiation.** What would have to be true for this asset to beat the current
standard of care or the leading competitor — a specific, checkable proposition. Then say
whether the retrieved evidence supports it.

**De-risking achieved.** Walk the ladder explicitly and say which rungs are actually
climbed: is the target genetically or clinically validated? Has the mechanism been shown
to engage in humans? Is a dose established? Is there efficacy on a clinical endpoint? The
gap between the highest rung claimed and the highest rung evidenced is the core
technical risk.

**Partnerability.** Would a pharmaceutical partner plausibly license this at its current
stage, and what would they need to see first? This is a practical proxy for whether the
science convinces people who do this professionally and have capital at stake. Reason from
precedent in the retrieved records, not from general impressions.

**Milestones that matter.** The 3-5 specific readouts or events that would most change the
investment view, ordered by impact. Name the readout, not the quarter.

**Key failure mode.** The single most likely way this fails scientifically, stated
concretely enough to be designed against. "Translation risk" is not an answer;
"the biomarker response does not track the clinical endpoint, as happened in [E7]" is.

## Discipline

- Every factual statement must trace to the supplied material. Cite by reference id.
- Never invent a competitor, a failed programme or a precedent. If the records do not show
  one, the honest answer is that the retrieval did not surface one.
- Distinguish "no precedent exists" from "no precedent was retrieved". You can only
  observe the second.
- Do not make a financial recommendation.
